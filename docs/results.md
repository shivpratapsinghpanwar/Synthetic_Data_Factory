## Interpretation (2026-08-23, first full experiment)

Read against the rule above (a delta is meaningful only when it clearly
exceeds both its spread and the class's per-image quantum):

- **Macro metrics: no effect.** macro-F1 +0.005 +/- 0.019 and accuracy
  -0.002 +/- 0.010 are both consistent with zero.
- **vasc (augmented): consistent F1 improvement.** +0.050 +/- 0.013 - the one
  augmented class where the effect exceeds its spread across all seeds.
- **bcc (augmented): small consistent degradation.** recall -0.052 +/- 0.013
  (~4 test images), F1 -0.031 +/- 0.015. Synthetic bcc slightly hurt bcc.
- **akiec, df (augmented): noise.** Spreads dwarf the means; df's 17-image
  test support cannot resolve an effect of this size.
- **Strongest consistent effect is off-target: melanoma recall
  +0.116 +/- 0.048** - positive in all three seeds, ~20x the per-image
  quantum (n=169) - paired with nv (majority class) recall -0.031 +/- 0.008.
  Adding rare-class synthetic data shifted decision boundaries away from the
  majority class, increasing sensitivity on disease classes. Melanoma
  sensitivity is the clinically critical metric in this domain, but the
  honest attribution is a class-balance effect, not melanoma-specific realism
  (mel was never augmented).

**Verdict:** the factory works end to end - generation passed memorization
gates, augmentation is provenance-tracked, and the measured effect is real
but mixed and subtle at 100 images/class over 3 seeds. Before any stronger
claim: more synthetic volume per class, more seeds, and a
class-balance-matched control (e.g. oversampling real rares) to separate the
balance effect from the realism effect.
